Football, not a random scoreboard.

Friday Night simulates field position, down and distance, clock runoff, strategic play calls, punts, field goals, turnovers, touchdowns and tries. Ratings shift probabilities; a seeded random generator decides outcomes. All displayed statistics are reductions of saved events. Fictional rosters remain consistent throughout each game.

Rules and scope

Default regulation uses four 12-minute quarters, three timeouts per half, 20-yard-line touchbacks and kicks after scores. Overtime uses paired possessions from the opponent’s 10, alternating the first offense each round. The ball is dead on a defensive change of possession in overtime. A winning second-possession touchdown ends the game without an unnecessary try. Custom 20/25-yard starts allow first downs. No college-style two-point shootout is used.

This is a probabilistic football model, not an officiating system or a prediction service. Penalties model false starts, offside and accepted holding with replayed downs; offsetting fouls, live-ball penalty choices, injuries, blocked kicks, fair-catch kicks and every state's mercy-rule variations are not modeled. Missed field goals are modeled as unreturned end-zone kicks. Kickoff returns are simplified, including free kicks after safeties. Sacks count as quarterback rushing losses. Weather, defensive strategy, fatigue, home advantage and late-game pressure modify probabilities. Win probability is a labeled heuristic, not a calibrated forecast.

Reference: IHSAA overtime procedure · High school quarter length. Check your state association before treating custom settings as an exact rules match.
